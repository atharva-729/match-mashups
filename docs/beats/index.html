Match Mashups · Beats

Music written around the action.

Stock music never lands on the moment. Can a generated soundtrack put every hit on the exact frame where it happens, and tell the clip's story like a dance track?

  • Find the moments on the picture, frame by frame: racket contact, the kick, ignition. Never the sound, which lags the picture by up to 0.14 s.
  • One steady tempo. The footage is nudged a few percent so each moment lands on a beat.
  • Build like a dance track: more notes as tension rises, one beat of silence, then the drop on the payoff.
  • Pay it off: a full drop, a slow-motion replay, and a final hit on the replayed moment.

What the numbers don't show

  • The sound lies. Broadcast audio arrives 65–140 ms after the picture, and for the far tennis player the loudest sound is often the bounce, not the racket. Hits placed on the sound feel late. Every hit here sits on a frame checked by eye.
  • Silence is the loudest beat. One beat of nothing (only the crowd, or nothing at all) right before the drop is what makes the drop land.
  • The music has to lead. Commentary sounded on top even at 30% of the energy: a voice stays clear when it's quiet. It now sits about 13 LU under the music, or it's removed.
  • A cut is a perfect hit. A rocket lifts off gradually, with no single frame. So the drop goes on a hard cut, which changes on an exact frame.
That is the result. Everything below is the how.

The pipeline

  1. Events from the picture. Frame strips, motion and colour measures, ball and rocket tracking; each hit checked against the frames before and after (ball in, ball out).
  2. Tempo and map. One tempo for the clip; each stretch between two events gets a whole number of beats and a speed nudge (at most ~10%, usually under 5%). Cuts land on beats; the payoff lands on a bar's first beat.
  3. The score. Written by code, note by note: tuned booms on each hit, a pulse, a pad, then the build (snare roll, riser), one beat of silence, and the drop (kick, clap, 808, pumping synth chords, lead melody, house piano, strings, brass, vocal-style chops), then the slow-motion replay and the final hit.
  4. Mix and render. Original sound (crowd, commentary) under the music or removed; video rendered with the map, captions and a flash on each big hit.

Rules learned from feedback

  • Put hits on the picture, never the sound.
  • The key moment of the clip must be exact; check it by following the ball or the object, not by guessing a delay.
  • A drop that just ends doesn't pay off: extend 5–10 s with a full drop, a slow-motion replay and a final hit.
  • Music in front; commentary well under it, or off.

Caveats

  • Tennis and NFL clips use broadcast footage and are shown as a demonstration only. The Apollo 11 film is public domain.
  • No AI music model and no samples: every sound is synthesised by code, so each note can be placed to the millisecond.
  • Hit timing is exact to the sample in the music; event frames are as exact as the footage allows (about ±1 frame where the action is tiny or far away).
